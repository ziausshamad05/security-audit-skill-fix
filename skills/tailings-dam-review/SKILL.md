# Tailings Dam Review

A disciplined way to review a TSF using the documents and data the user provides. It finds gaps and questions; it does **not** certify stability or replace the Engineer of Record (EoR), an independent review board, or the regulator.

## Required inputs (ask if missing)
- Facility name/type, raising method (upstream / downstream / centerline / single-stage), height, stored volume, age, current status (active / closed).
- Downstream setting: people, infrastructure, rivers, distance.
- Available documents: design basis, site investigation, stability analyses, seepage/water balance, monitoring data, inspection reports, dam safety review, emergency plan.
- Jurisdiction and applicable regulation (confirm; in Indonesia, mining engineering good-practice rules from the energy and mineral resources ministry apply and should be verified for the current version).

## Method
1. **Consequence classification.** Check that a failure-consequence class exists (GISTM scale: Low, Significant, High, Very High, Extreme) and that the design criteria match it, including population at risk and environmental impact.
2. **GISTM gap check.** Walk the six topics: affected communities; integrated knowledge base; design, construction, operation and monitoring; management and governance (accountable executive, Responsible Tailings Facility Engineer, EoR, independent review); emergency response and long-term recovery; public disclosure and access to information. Mark each: evidenced / partial / missing.
3. **Failure-mode screen.** For each, ask what evidence addresses it: overtopping (freeboard, flood and beach length), slope instability, foundation failure, static or seismic liquefaction (especially upstream-raised dams), internal erosion/piping, seepage and phreatic surface, erosion of the embankment, structural failure of decant or spillway, and loss of water-balance control.
4. **Monitoring red flags.** Rising piezometric levels or phreatic surface, new or increasing seepage/turbid seepage, settlement or crest deformation trends, inclinometer or InSAR movement, reduced beach width or freeboard, instrument gaps, and alert thresholds exceeded or missing. Check that triggers (TARP) exist and are followed.
5. **Emergency preparedness.** Dam-break study, inundation map, communication to downstream communities, drills, and clear triggers.
6. **Prioritize.** Rank gaps by consequence × likelihood evidence; mark anything suggesting imminent instability as **urgent: escalate to the EoR and accountable manager immediately**.

## Output format
**Facility summary** · **Consequence class (stated / missing)**
**GISTM gap table:** topic | evidence | status | action
**Failure-mode table:** mode | evidence reviewed | concern | question
**Monitoring red flags** · **Priority actions (urgent / near-term / longer-term)**
**Documents not seen / assumptions.**

> A qualified geotechnical engineer, the EoR, the independent review panel and the regulator are the authority. This review identifies questions and gaps only and must not be used as a statement that a dam is safe.

## Anti-patterns
- Stating a dam is "stable" or "safe" from a document review.
- Ignoring the raising method (upstream construction needs extra scrutiny for liquefaction and drainage).
- Treating missing monitoring data as "no problem".
- Quoting regulatory clauses or numeric criteria from memory; ask for the document.
